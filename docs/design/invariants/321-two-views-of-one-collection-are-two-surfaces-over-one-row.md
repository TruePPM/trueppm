# Rule 321 — Two "views" of one collection are two SURFACES over one row model, and a surface may choose columns — never …

> **Invariant.** Indexed from [`packages/web/CLAUDE.md`](../../../packages/web/CLAUDE.md), section *Dialogs, focus and keyboard*. The index carries the rule's headline; this file carries its full text (#3744, ADR-0653 amendment). Binding everywhere, not only on the surface that produced it.

**Two "views" of one collection are two SURFACES over one row model, and a surface may choose columns — never rows.** The Schedule shipped Grid and Timeline as a segmented toggle, and Timeline did not render the outline at all: it hid `TaskListPanel` and let the canvas paint task names itself, into a frozen 176px column it drew with its own truncation, its own row loop and its own idea of what a summary row looks like (#1221, #2096). Nothing was *wrong* on either surface, which is why it survived a year — the cost is silent and cumulative. Every piece of row chrome the outline grew afterwards existed on exactly one of them: the depth guides and phase band (#2956), the delivery-mode gutter and chip (#2737), the ⋮⋮ reorder grip (#2954), the three insert affordances (#2957), the structural-undo trail (#2974). A planner who switched layout to see the bars better lost the ability to see what contained what, and there was no moment at which anybody decided that. Four obligations. **(a) One call site, one derivation, fed one array.** The fix is structural, not disciplinary: render the *same component* on both surfaces from the same rows and the same fold state, so a surface **cannot** re-derive row identity, order, containment or expansion because it is never asked to. "Keep them in sync" is not an obligation you can discharge — a second `flattenVisible` will drift, and the drift's symptom is a missing affordance rather than an error. **(b) What a surface chooses is presentation, and the choice is a pure function of the surface.** `surfaceColumnVisibility(surface, visible)` narrows the user's persisted column set; it **narrows and never widens**, so a column hidden on one surface cannot reappear on the other, and there is nowhere for a surface-specific row filter to hide. Anything derived from that choice — the panel's width, the Display menu's Columns section, the overlay offsets — resolves through the same predicate, so the menu that offers a column and the panel that draws it cannot disagree (rule 316 applied to a layout). **(c) When the second surface arrives, DELETE what compensated for its absence, in the same MR.** The canvas name gutter existed only because "the task table is hidden (Timeline view)" — its own docstring said so. Leaving it once the outline renders on both gives you two frozen name columns side by side and, worse, a Display option that changes nothing; a retired option must come out of the menu, out of the persisted allow-list, and out of the renderer, with the stored preference falling back to the *closest surviving behavior* rather than to the list's first entry. **(d) Pin the invariant with a test that renders both surfaces and compares.** Assert the row ids, `aria-level` and `aria-expanded` are identical while the `columnheader` count differs — that is the one assertion a future Timeline-specific row list fails, and no test of either surface alone can make it. Corollary: geometry that was slack on the wide surface becomes load-bearing on the narrow one, so re-check the bounds you never needed — an unclamped splitter drag merely looked untidy beside five data columns and pushes the entire bar track off screen when the outline is the only thing to its left. Reference: `features/schedule/scheduleSurface.ts`, `features/schedule/ScheduleView.tsx` (`ScheduleMainArea`, `PanelSplitter`), `features/schedule/scheduleSurface.test.tsx`, `e2e/schedule-view-mode.spec.ts` — #2960, from `TruePPM - Designer Prototype.html` "The timeline is the grid".
