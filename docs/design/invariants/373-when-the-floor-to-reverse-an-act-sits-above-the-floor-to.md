# Rule 373 — When the floor to REVERSE an act sits ABOVE the floor to perform it, the receipt must carry the server's …

> **Invariant.** Indexed from [`packages/web/CLAUDE.md`](../../../packages/web/CLAUDE.md), section *Surface states*. The index carries the rule's headline; this file carries its full text (#3744, ADR-0653 amendment). Binding everywhere, not only on the surface that produced it.

**When the floor to REVERSE an act sits ABOVE the floor to perform it, the receipt must carry the server's verdict on the reversal — never infer it from the act having succeeded.** A 200 on the write says the caller cleared the *write's* gate and nothing whatever about the undo's. Applying a classification cascade and pasting many rows are both `IsProjectPlanAuthor` (Member+ minus the resource band) while `/cascade-classification-operations/{id}/undo/` and `/paste-many-operations/{id}/undo/` are Admin+; a CSV import is `IsProjectScheduler` (200) while its undo is Admin+ (300). Three surfaces independently attached an Undo keyed on the operation-ledger handle alone, and each offered a control that 403s (#3304). Four obligations. **(a) The apply response carries a capability boolean computed by the SAME predicate the undo endpoint enforces** — `access.permissions.role_can_undo_batch_operation`, the ADR-0133 "one rule, called twice" pattern `can_author` already follows. Not a client-side `role >= ROLE_ADMIN`: the client copy drifts, it cannot see an Enterprise custom role registered in the 301–399 band, and on the product backlog it gets the answer *wrong* — that entry point is gated on `can_manage_backlog`, which is `Admin+ OR the Product Owner facet`, so a PO below Admin may apply and may not undo, and no ordinal comparison distinguishes them. It also rides the very response that raises the affordance, so unlike a `useCurrentUserRole` read it can never be unsettled, stale, or racing at the moment the decision is made. **(b) Keep it ORTHOGONAL to the ledger handle.** `operation_id` answers "is there anything to undo"; the capability answers "may you". Fold them into one boolean and a `false` means either, with no way for a caller to tell them apart — the null-meaning-two-things trap. The client requires both. **(c) OMIT the control, do not disable it** (rule 302, ux-review §6.1). A disabled button inside an auto-dismissing toast announces "Undo, dimmed" with no reachable explanation, because the hover-or-focus reveal a tooltip needs is a race the user loses. Absence is strictly better. **(d) Rule 302's silence clause does NOT transfer — decide the disclosure deliberately, and put it BEFORE the act.** 302 stays silent because nothing was on offer; here the user *was* offered the act and is now holding a result they alone cannot reverse. The toast is the wrong host — an 8s `aria-live` region cannot carry a second clause — so if the asymmetry is worth saying, say it on the standing surface that commits the act, and make the docs state the recovery route either way. **Done in #3357** — `ClassificationPopover`'s footer note, keyed on `Project.can_undo_batch_operations` (the same predicate, on a payload the surface holds *before* the act). Two rules fall out of it and govern any repeat: **379** for the tri-state default a disclosure needs, and **380** for binding the description where a keyboard commit can actually reach it. **Test at the RECEIPT, not the endpoint**: the endpoints always refused correctly, so a pytest on the 403 passes on the broken build. Note the two surfaces do not share a toast — `ScheduleView` renders its own via `setScheduleActionToast`, the backlog routes through the global `ToastHost` — so a hook-level test pins the decision and only a per-surface test proves it renders. Reference: `hooks/useTaskClassification.ts` (`can_undo`), `features/schedule/classification/useClassificationPopover.ts`, `features/schedule/SeedBanner.tsx` (the omit precedent, still on a client ordinal). **Four of five surfaces now follow this rule — #3353 closed the last two that shipped the defect.** The fifth, `features/schedule/SeedBanner.tsx`, is the *omit* precedent and still gates on a client ordinal (`currentRole >= ROLE_ADMIN`), which obligation (a) forbids: it cannot see an Enterprise band role and it misses the archived-project and retention-window refusals. Its server counterpart (`template_views`'s `undo`) moved onto the shared predicate in #3353, so the field it should read is the only piece still missing — tracked in #3355's neighborhood, not closed here. `features/schedule/buildMode/pasteMany/PasteReceiptStrip.tsx` takes `can_undo` off the `tasks/bulk/` 207 and `components/import/CsvImportWizard.tsx` takes it off the import status payload. Two things generalize from them. **(e) Withholding the control is only half the fix when a CHORD is also bound to it** — the strip advertised `⌘Z` in its own label and `ScheduleView` bound it, and the wizard's `claimUndoShortcut()` took the rule-307 claim away from `SeedBanner`, which gates correctly; so a surface that omits its button and keeps its binding still teaches a chord that 403s, and one that keeps its *claim* breaks a working shortcut on a sibling to fail at its own. Unbind and un-claim on the same term. And when the receipt is the ONLY claimant, leave the chord unbound rather than falling through to a different destructive undo — `ScheduleView`'s structural `mod+z` is deliberately NOT the else-branch of the paste receipt's gate. **(f) 373(d)'s silence was a property of the TOAST, not of the rule.** The objection there was structural: an 8s `aria-live` region cannot carry a second clause, and a tooltip's hover-or-focus reveal is a race the reader loses. Both of these surfaces persist until the author acts, so both carry the disclosure inline — name the recovery route, not just the refusal (the rows are ordinary tasks the caller may still delete). Keep it off the paths where nothing was on offer: a FAILED import says nothing about the undo floor (rule 302's silence clause). Two rules govern the mechanics of that disclosure and are not optional here: **379** for the tri-state default (the SAME field drives an affordance and a disclosure, whose safe defaults are opposite) and **386** for keeping it inside the focus-seating predicate when it renders outside a live region. Non-undo controls on the same surface stay — **Keep** and **Map columns…** are not undos, and gating them would turn one missing capability into three. Whether the Admin+ undo floor is itself right is a separate open question — `structural_operation_services` deliberately implements actor-or-Admin instead (#3355).
