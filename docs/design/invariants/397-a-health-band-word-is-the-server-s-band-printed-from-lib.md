# Rule 397 — A health band word is the SERVER's band, printed from `lib/healthBand`'s `HEALTH_BAND_LABEL` — never a …

> **Invariant.** Indexed from [`packages/web/CLAUDE.md`](../../../packages/web/CLAUDE.md), section *Signal encoding*. The index carries the rule's headline; this file carries its full text (#3744, ADR-0653 amendment). Binding everywhere, not only on the surface that produced it.

**A health band word is the SERVER's band, printed from `lib/healthBand`'s `HEALTH_BAND_LABEL` — never a surface-private synonym, and never re-derived from counts by a surface whose payload could have carried the band.** Rule 7 governs health *color*; it is silent on the word and on which value the word comes from, which is the gap this fills. Two failures, and the second outlives fixing the first. **(a) The synonym.** The shell health chip mapped the three bands onto words of its own — "At risk" for the *critical* band, "On watch" for *at-risk* — so the top bar and the page beneath it printed different words for one project, and "On watch" was a word no ADR, design record or other surface defined (#3470). Minting a fourth word for a three-value vocabulary is exactly what ADR-0126's "one status vocabulary" forbids; a task-count nuance belongs in the popover rows, not in the word. **(b) The re-derivation — and there is now NO client-side way to do it.** `views.py::compute_health_band` checks the manual `Project.health` override **before** the counts, so a client deriving a band from `at_risk_count` / `critical_count` alone is correct only until a PM sets a status by hand — and it fails *quietly*, printing a plausible word rather than an error, which is why no spec on the word can see it. That is exactly what happened: the shell chip printed "On track" over a project its own PM had reported Critical, and "Critical" over one reported On track, on every route where the top bar is the only health reading (#3501). **Superseded 2026-09-07 (!MR for #3501):** this clause used to carve out `deriveHealthBand()` as legitimate "for the one payload that genuinely carries no band (`status-summary`)". That carve-out was the bug — the right move was never to derive more carefully but to widen the endpoint. `status-summary` now returns `health_band` from the same `compute_health_band` the my-projects triage list calls, `deriveHealthBand()` is **deleted**, and `lib/healthBand.ts` deliberately exports no way to compute a band at all. Do not reintroduce one: if a new payload cannot carry a band, add the field to that payload. **When a surface must print a band, prefer widening its endpoint to return `health_band` over deriving it client-side** — the authoritative classification of a server-computed outcome is itself server-computed (rule 301(a), ADR-0599). **(c) Corollary for width-regression specs, and it is the half that gets inverted.** A chrome-clipping fixture (rule 290) must pin the **widest reachable** label, and widest is **measured, not counted, and not inferred from severity**. Measured in Chromium against the bundled Inter at `text-xs font-medium`: "On track" **48.73px**, "Critical" **40.55px**, "At risk" **36.47px** — eight characters each for the first and second, and 8.2px apart. `mobile-chrome-clip` and `unified-shell-bar` both pinned the *critical* band, which was right only while the at-risk band rendered the retired "On watch" (54.48px); carrying that fixture forward on the assumption that the worst health is the widest word would have narrowed the guard while its comment claimed it widened it — and the fixture it was hiding was a real clip, the account chip pushed to 377.20px on a 375px screen. Measure the candidates **in a browser, single-threaded**, and write the figures into the fixture's comment so the next author inherits the numbers rather than the inference. **Record the container's slack beside the word widths, or the table you leave behind reads as self-contradictory**: at 375px a 12.26px wider word costs only 2px of overflow, because a `flex-nowrap` header absorbs ~10px in its compressible neighbours and pins `scrollWidth` at `clientWidth` until it runs out — a reviewer who sees only the word widths will (correctly) call the numbers impossible. **And a parallel worktree makes every one of these measurements untrustworthy by default**: Playwright's `reuseExistingServer` is `!CI`, so a `vite preview` another worktree left on 4173 is silently reused and you measure *its* bundle. Run width work on a private port. **Superseded in part 2026-09-07 (#3505, merged as !2382):** `mobile-chrome-clip` no longer pins a band at all — it sweeps a `BANDS` matrix (on_track / at_risk / critical) and re-asserts the geometry per row, because pinning *whichever word is widest today* only moves the blind spot to the next relabeling, which is the inversion this clause opens by warning about. So: pin the widest label only where the fixture must genuinely choose one band (`unified-shell-bar` still does, on `on_track`); where the band is a free input, sweep it and let nothing below the fixture read the word. The measurements above stay — they are what proves `on_track`, not the worst severity, is the widest — and a swept band must travel in the payload as `health_band`, because the chip stopped deriving one from the counts (b): omit it and all three rows render "On track" through the `?? 'on_track'` fallback, testing one band three times under a comment claiming three. **(d) Note the mechanism honestly (rule 300).** `HEALTH_BAND_LABEL` is a plain `Record<HealthBand, string>`, so nothing stops the next surface inlining a literal at the TYPE level — this clause was a convention, not a gate, and weaker than the two prior fixes of this same class: #3031's `rowVocabulary.ts` mints a branded `VocabularyToken` that makes a literal in a governed prop a **compile error**, backed by `scripts/check-row-vocabulary.sh` / CI `web:row-vocabulary`, and #3023 did the same for the four dependency link terms. **#3502 closed the population, not the type gap**: the seven surfaces that carried private band maps (SCREAMING keys, kebab keys, the manual-override enum) now map their own key shape onto `HEALTH_BAND_LABEL` at the edge, and `lib/healthBand.conformance.test.ts` (rule 414) fails any *other* module whose source declares an object literal restating two or more of the three band words as its own values. Be precise about what that buys: it is a source-scan, not a compile-time gate — a paraphrase ("Healthy" for on-track), a single-word restatement, or a value built by concatenation rather than a literal would not be caught, the same honest gap rule 395 names for message families. It is real coverage of the *known* failure mode (a copy-pasted literal map, which is how all seven and the shell chip itself went wrong) and no more than that. The branded-token treatment #3031/#3023 used remains the stronger fix and is not built here; do a mechanism-vs-scope check before claiming this rule is airtight. Reference: `lib/healthBand.ts`, `features/shell/HealthCluster.tsx`, `hooks/useShellStats.ts`, `e2e/mobile-chrome-clip.spec.ts`, `e2e/shell-health-band.spec.ts` — #3470 and #3501; the private-label-map sweep and its conformance test are #3502, the 2px phone overflow #3505.
