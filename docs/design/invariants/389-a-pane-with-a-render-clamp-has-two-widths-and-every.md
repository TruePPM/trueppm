# Rule 389 — A pane with a RENDER clamp has two widths, and every consumer must be told which one it is reading — the …

> **Invariant.** Indexed from [`packages/web/CLAUDE.md`](../../../packages/web/CLAUDE.md), section *Accessibility floors*. The index carries the rule's headline; this file carries its full text (#3744, ADR-0653 amendment). Binding everywhere, not only on the surface that produced it.

**A pane with a RENDER clamp has two widths, and every consumer must be told which one it is reading — the width the pane ASKED for positions nothing.** Rule 370 gave the Schedule outline a clamp: it paints at `outlinePaneWidthFor(...)` with `overflow-hidden` and clips its rightmost columns rather than let the bar track fall under its floor. What the clamp did not do is reach the surfaces hanging off the outline's right edge. `ScheduleLegend` and `UnscheduledGutter` are `paddingLeft`-ed by `panelWidth` — the *summed column* width — so once the clamp fires they indent past the canvas by exactly the amount the outline was clipped. This was already wrong before it was noticeable: at 1280 with the rail expanded the eight-column outline asked for 738px against a 708px budget, and the tray sat 30px out for the life of the feature with nobody filing it. Adding two columns took the gap to 142 and pushed the tray's cards off the right edge (#3344). Four obligations. **(a) Name the two quantities differently at the point they diverge** — `panelWidth` (asked) vs `overlayAnchorWidth` (given) — because a single `panelWidth` passed to five call sites is five silent assumptions that it means the visible edge. **(b) Derive the anchor where the measurement lives, and pass it down.** The clamp needs the observed container width, which only the component holding the `ResizeObserver` has; a sibling that receives `panelWidth` as a prop cannot correct it, which is why the milestone pulse still carries the defect and is tracked separately rather than fixed by guessing. **(c) The failure surfaces as unrelated tests, never as a layout bug.** Six gutter/backlog E2E specs failed on `toBeVisible()` for a card whose only problem was that it had been translated off screen; not one of them mentions a column, a width, or the outline. When a change to a pane's width reds specs on a surface it does not touch, the padding is the first thing to read. **(d) A pre-existing offset is a finding, not a baseline** (rule 366(d) again, from the other side) — the 30px was shipping, and treating it as "how it has always looked" is what let two columns turn it into a broken tray.
- **Corollary, for the specs: a magic number that COMPENSATES for a layout constant inherits a premise from a product default, and the next default breaks it with a failure that names nothing.** `schedule-drag-dependency-arrow.spec.ts` set a 1920 viewport in `beforeEach` "so both bars stay comfortably inside the visible canvas", then dragged a bar `+200px` from its centre. The outline is `flex-shrink-0` and the canvas `flex-1`, so both numbers were really statements about how wide the outline happened to be; two columns took 112px off the canvas, the auto-fitted bar narrowed from 32.6px to 28.7px, and its centre crossed `GanttHitIndex`'s `barRight - 16` resize threshold. The gesture became a **resize**, a "Resize task?" popover opened, and the spec failed asserting the *Reschedule* dialog was missing — with a correct-looking dialog on screen the whole time. It had been passing by **0.3px**. Derive such a number from the thing it is compensating for (measure the canvas; aim at `barLeft + 3`, which is body at any width), and assert the derived value is big enough to be non-vacuous rather than trusting it silently.
