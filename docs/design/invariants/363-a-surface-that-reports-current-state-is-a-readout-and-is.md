# Rule 363 — A surface that REPORTS current state is a readout and is exempt; everything else is a teaching surface, and …

> **Invariant.** Indexed from [`packages/web/CLAUDE.md`](../../../packages/web/CLAUDE.md), section *Redundancy*. The index carries the rule's headline; this file carries its full text (#3744, ADR-0653 amendment). Binding everywhere, not only on the surface that produced it.

**A surface that REPORTS current state is a readout and is exempt; everything else is a teaching surface, and at most ONE teaching surface renders per column (#3134).** Rule 284 is about a *value* rendering twice. This is the same defect one level up, about a *lesson*: on a build-mode, edit-rights, row-focused Schedule, `ScheduleCoachBar`, `BuildModeHintStrip` and `ScheduleInsertTargetStatement` all drew at once, and a user reading three hint bands reads none of them. **Measure the geometry before you reason about it** — both the issue and the design placed the coach bar *above* the outline, and it is not there: measured at 1920×1080 it draws at `y=978` (29px) and the hint strip at `y=979` (28px), so the two share one slot beneath the plan and were stacking to ~57px of hint bars. That fact strengthens the case rather than weakening it, and a rule argued from the wrong one would have been argued from prose. Apply four clauses **in order**, and stop at the first that decides:

**(a) Classify.** Is the string a pure function of current state? Then it is a **readout**, it teaches nothing, and it leaves the arbitration entirely. The insert-target statement is a readout — it names where `+ Item` will land — which is why the count of three was right about the pixels and wrong about the kinds. **A control is a readout too**: a button that performs the act and prints its chord beside it (the strip's `Edit N items` front door, #3152) is not competing to teach, it is offering to do. Getting this clause wrong is how a proposal ends up deleting the only *teacher* in favour of a surface that was never teaching.

**(b) Actionability.** Can the lesson be performed *here, now*? A coach that teaches indent, group and hover over a plan with **zero visible rows** is teaching three acts with nothing to act on, while `BlankOutlineDraftRow` — the live row already holding the caret (#2733) — is teaching the one act that is available, from a narrower anchor. Suppress it. Note the predicate is the **visible** row count, not the total: a filter that hides every row leaves the same empty canvas and the same unperformable lessons.

**(c) Anchor.** Where two surfaces both pass (a) and (b) and their content overlaps, the **narrower anchor wins** — control > region > venue. The hint strip is anchored to focus state and selection size; the coach bar is anchored to the venue (build mode is on). So the strip wins wherever both would draw.

**(d) One per column.** Equal anchors do not co-present: they **merge**, or the later one becomes **on-demand behind a persistent labelled control**. `BuildModeCheatsheet` (~40 chips, opened by `?`) is the compliant end state of that fallback and is a teaching surface in good standing — name it in any census of this column; it never competes because it is never on-screen unasked. **Deletion is only ever for a true duplicate**, and see the corollary below for what "true" costs to establish.

**The corollary, which is the half that gets skipped: before retiring any surface, name what ELSE it carries — accessible description, live region, focus target, the sole discovery point for a chord — and transfer those jobs by name in the same change.** The #3134 design proposed deleting the hint strip because its content was "already duplicated, so no string is lost". Checked against the component, three of its four hint sets had no counterpart anywhere, and `SELECTION_HINTS` was the only place the group chord is advertised at the moment a multi-row selection exists (#2955) — load-bearing precisely because `displayOptions.structureButtons` ships off. **Two failure modes, and the second is the trap**: the obvious one is deleting a surface that carries an untransferred job; the subtler one is deduplicating the *wrong* line, because the line that looks redundant is often the only one that is not. Establish the overlap by reading both components, string by string, and write the table down — a claim of duplication sourced from prose is not evidence.

**Prefer a partition to a suppression term.** Where the clauses yield mutually exclusive predicates, spell them as one — the coach bar renders in `NoSelection`, the strip in everything else — so "never both" is provable by enumeration rather than asserted by two `&&` chains in different parts of a 6,000-line component. **Suppression is a render condition and never a write**: a bar that stands down for the moment must not clear the stored option that dismissed it, or "restore" restores nothing and the one-way dismissal of #2959 is back. Reference: `features/schedule/buildMode/teachingSurfaces.ts` and its test, which asserts the invariant over the whole input space.
