# Rule 343 — A fixed-height control strip that cannot wrap and must not scroll needs a MEASURED concession ladder, and the …

> **Invariant.** Indexed from [`packages/web/CLAUDE.md`](../../../packages/web/CLAUDE.md), section *Signal encoding*. The index carries the rule's headline; this file carries its full text (#3744, ADR-0653 amendment). Binding everywhere, not only on the surface that produced it.

**A fixed-height control strip that cannot wrap and must not scroll needs a MEASURED concession ladder, and the ladder's rungs are two different kinds of thing.** Rule 290 covers the strip that is allowed to scroll; this is its sibling for the strip that is not — a toolbar whose popovers are in-flow `absolute` panels, where `overflow-x: auto` would clip every one of them (rule 290's own overlay corollary) and would make a control's location depend on a scroll offset nothing announces. The Schedule toolbar's natural width is ~1,862px against 752–1,648px of room, so it was over budget at **every** supported width and the parent's `overflow-hidden` ate the difference in silence: `Export PDF`, the `···` trigger and everything inside it were painted nowhere and reachable by nothing — not a pointer, not a scroll, not a Tab (#3076). Six obligations. **(a) Measure; do not guess with a media query.** Natural width depends on the project's own strings, on the rail being collapsed, on edit rights, and on the user's pins — a `useBreakpoint()` tier can see none of that, and every width it guessed wrong clipped without a symptom. A `ResizeObserver` + a pure `nextFitStep(step, contentWidth, availableWidth, costs)` walked until it fits, with **hysteresis** (a rung is only given back once there is its own cost *plus* a margin to spare) is what stops a drag-resize oscillating. **(b) `scrollWidth` cannot measure a strip with a `flex-1` spacer** — the spacer absorbs all slack, so `scrollWidth === clientWidth` at every width and the loop can never climb back. Sum the children and charge the spacer its floor. **(c) Every child needs `shrink-0`.** One child without it absorbs the entire overflow alone and wraps its label inside the fixed height — which is both the visible bug and the thing that hides the overflow from the measurement. **(d) Rungs are collapses or demotions, and you spend every collapse first.** A **collapse** keeps the control in the bar showing its value and costs a glance; a **demotion** puts it behind a click and costs a hunt. They are not comparable by pixels saved, so ordering by cost interleaves them wrongly — and it is what made the design's own ladder demote a control that a prior issue (#2703) had deliberately promoted *out* of the overflow. **(e) Four behaviours, not two: a command may hide, a readout may only shorten, a mode may only collapse into something that still shows its value, and tier-A demotes last or never.** Treating a Read/Author indicator as an overflowable command is how someone types into a plan they believe is read-only, so give it no `overflow` state to reach. **And check whether the mode needs a rung at all: if the collapsed form is the right control, ship only that.** The Schedule carried `BuildModePill` *and* the Read/Author pill at wide widths and merged them into one chip at narrow ones — so a user asking "am I allowed to edit this" got two answers in two places above a width threshold and one below it, with nothing on screen saying how the two composed. The narrow form was the team having already found the collision; #3263 applied it everywhere, which deleted a rung **and** took the same px off the bar's natural width, so the ladder lost nothing. The tell that a mode control is really two: one half of its value is a constant on the surface where it renders (`buildModeActive` is `!isMobile`, and the chip only renders on desktop, so "Build off" was text that could never appear) — #3263. **(f) A demoted control keeps its name and `aria-keyshortcuts`, appears in exactly one place, and its menu heading states WHY it moved** — "no room at this width" is a false sentence about a control the user unpinned, and a heading that lies about the reason sends them resizing the window for something only a settings menu can return. Two corollaries. The user-facing half belongs in the surface's own Display popover, where each row states *where the control is right now* and the footer counts pins honestly ("2 of 4 fit at this width"), because that is the only place in the product that can answer "where did my button go" — and a pin is a request, never a guarantee: nothing may clip to honour one. And **an unmeasurable strip is not a cramped strip** — jsdom, a detached node and `display:none` all report 0, so a pessimistic first-paint seed held against a zero measurement parks the toolbar at its narrowest composition forever in every environment that never lays out. Testing follows rule 300's split exactly: jsdom can assert the ordering and the anti-oscillation property and nothing else, so the *measurement* is Playwright's — and `toBeVisible()` is not the assertion, because a clipped child of an `overflow-hidden` parent still has a box and still passes it (the #2974 lesson); assert each child's right edge against the bar's. Reference: `features/schedule/toolbar/toolbarLadder.ts` (the rungs, `nextFitStep`, `measureToolbarContent`), `toolbar/useToolbarFit.ts` (the loop and its `inventorySignature` — a pin toggle changes natural width without changing the box, which a `ResizeObserver` cannot see), `toolbar/useDemotionAnnounce.ts` (focus + a polite announcement when the focused control is the one demoted), `components/toolbar/ToolbarOverflowMenu.tsx` (sections), `e2e/schedule-toolbar-fit.spec.ts` — #3076.
