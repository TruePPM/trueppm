- **A long calendar exception no longer slows down scheduling as dependencies grow**: every dependency edge snaps a date to a working day in the forward pass, the backward pass, and the free-float calculation. Both scheduling engines used to walk those snaps one day at a time, so one valid exception range close to the 100-year scan limit (for example a "closed until further notice" entry) cost about 32 ms per edge. A 200-dependency project took 6 s to schedule and a 5,000-task one took minutes, all synchronously on the what-if and program-schedule endpoints. Snaps now skip a whole exception range in one step, and working-day spans are counted arithmetically, in both the Python engine and the Rust/WASM engine. The 5,000-dependency case now takes under 0.1 s, and scheduled dates are unchanged (#4161).
