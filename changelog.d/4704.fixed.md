The scheduler now reports a link as driving whenever it binds its successor, that is, when the date the link imposes, read in the successor's working time, is the successor's early date. A link with zero relationship free float is still reported as driving too. Before, `driving_edges` (and the `is_driving` flag on each dependency) tested only for zero relationship free float, counted in the predecessor's working days. Two kinds of link that alone held their successor were left out, so the successor showed no driving predecessor:

- A link from a wider calendar into a narrower one, such as a seven-day-week task finishing on a Friday into a Monday-to-Friday task that starts on Monday.
- A link whose calendar-day lag lands on non-working time ([#4537](https://gitlab.com/trueppm/trueppm/-/issues/4537)).

Free float is unchanged and stays a separate output. Such a predecessor can still report days of free float while driving its successor. Both the Python and the WASM engine apply the same rule, so the schedule view weights these links as driving after the next recompute.
