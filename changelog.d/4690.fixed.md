- **Scheduler: recording a milestone on the day it is shown no longer moves its successors** (#4690): marking a milestone done or started on the day the schedule showed it, or setting its `planned_start` to that day, could push its lagged successors and the project finish one or two working days later. This happened when the milestone sat at a midnight before the start of its shown day, such as Sunday 00:00 shown on Monday, or a point before the project start. A milestone closed after all of its predecessors are complete now links from the midnight it linked from live, read from those predecessors. A recorded `actual_start` on the shown day, or a `planned_start` on, or snapping to, that day, now leaves its successors where they were. A `planned_start` that snaps to the shown day (such as a Saturday or Sunday date before a Monday) is a change to live schedules, and an earlier-only one: it used to push the milestone's lagged successors later. Closing a milestone while one of its predecessors is still open can still move its lagged successors, as before (#4740). Both the Python and the Rust/WASM engines are fixed.
- **Scheduler: a data date on a milestone's shown day no longer moves its successors** (#4690). This changes live schedules. When the data date falls on, or snaps to, the day a link already shows a milestone at the start of, it used to become the instant the milestone's lagged successors counted from, and pushed them later. A Saturday or Sunday data date snaps to the following Monday and triggered it too. It now leaves them where the link puts them.
