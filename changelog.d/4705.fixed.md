- **Scheduler: a link between a summary task and its own descendants is now refused,
  not silently rewritten** (#4705, #4538): `expand_summary_dependencies` used to treat
  a link whose endpoints share a leaf (a summary linked to one of its own children, a
  child linked to its own parent, a summary linked to itself, or a nested summary
  linked to its ancestor) as a harmless self-reference. Depending on the shape it
  dropped the link, turned it into an invented order between sibling tasks
  (`S{a, b} → a` scheduled as `b → a`), or raised a cycle through two leaves nobody had
  linked. `find_cycle` reports every one of these inputs as a cycle, and the expansion
  now agrees with it: it raises `CyclicDependencyError` with `.cycle` naming the source
  link as `[predecessor, successor, predecessor]`, or `[id, id]` for a link to itself.
  A Start-to-Start or Start-to-Finish link *from* a summary still raises
  `InvalidScheduleInput` instead, because the ADR-0370 check runs first.
  **Library callers who passed such links straight to `expand_summary_dependencies`,
  without running `find_cycle` first, used to get a silently reordered or unconstrained
  schedule. They now get `CyclicDependencyError`.**
- **Indent, outdent and reparent refuse a move that would link a summary to its own
  subtask** (#4705): with `Design → Build` on adjacent rows, indenting Build under
  Design used to succeed and leave the project unable to recalculate. These three
  endpoints now return 400 with code `summary_descendant_link`, naming the links in
  `blocking_dependencies`, and change nothing. Saving a dependency already refused
  such a link, so both ways of creating one in the app are now closed. A project whose
  stored data already holds one (the MS Project, CSV and Jira importers do not check
  for it yet, #4856) fails its recalculation with the cycle error instead of
  producing a schedule that ignores the link.
