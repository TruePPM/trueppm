- **A milestone whose shown date hops a weekend no longer reads as a slip (#4178).** Since #4173, a milestone that a lag lands on or just after a weekend or holiday is shown at the start of the next working day, while one that follows work is shown at the end of its day. The end of a Friday and the start of the following Monday are the same point in working time, but several consumers subtracted the two shown dates as calendar days. So a schedule change that moved nothing in working time could email the project's managers that the end date slipped three days, send a "Forecast shifted" digest at sprint close, show "+3d later" on the sprint bridge card or "finish +3d" in the activity feed, or raise a cross-project slip conflict for a milestone that finishes on the sprint's last day. A real move could also be under-reported: the Monte Carlo what-if showed a one-day `cpm_finish` shift when the finish moved from the end of Friday to the end of Tuesday. The end-date shift notification, the milestone forecast digest, the bridge card, the activity feed's finish delta, the what-if `delta_vs_current.cpm_finish` and the sprint-boundary slip check now compare the finish in working time. They read a start-of-day milestone finish as the end of the working day before it. A real shift is still reported in calendar days. Project forecast snapshots (`GET /api/v1/projects/{id}/forecast-snapshots/`) and milestone forecasts (`GET /api/v1/projects/{id}/forecast/`, including `previous`) now carry `cpm_finish_at_day_start` (null on rows written before the upgrade), and each milestone forecast carries a server-computed `cpm_finish_shift_days`. Baseline drift, the milestone rollup variance and Monte Carlo run-to-run deltas still compare shown dates; that is tracked in #4197. The scheduler's `ScheduleResult` and `Task` docstrings and the Scheduler Conventions page now state this display rule.
